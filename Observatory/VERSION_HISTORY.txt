v117 — Speech capture pipeline: audio session tuning, crash fix, diarization threshold

Date: 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-15

This version is a sequence of real, hard-won tradeoffs in
SpeechRecognitionManager.swift's audio session configuration, not a single
feature. See ONBOARDING.md's "Recent history" section for the full
narrative; summary:

- Fixed weak mic capture (.measurement mode was disabling AGC) by
  switching to .default, then reverted back to .measurement once it became
  clear the AGC-enabled mode was hurting SFSpeechRecognizer's accuracy more
  than it helped perceived loudness.
- Added a one-time in-app nudge toward the system Voice Isolation Mic Mode
  for single-speaker-focused capture, after trying (then reverting) the
  audio engine's own Voice-Processing I/O, which double-processed the
  signal on top of system Mic Mode and measurably hurt recognition.
- Fixed an OSStatus -50 crash on start, encountered while the above
  voice-processing experiment was active (needs .playAndRecord, not
  .record, category) -- moot after the revert, documented anyway since the
  underlying category/voice-processing interaction is a real constraint.
- Added request.addsPunctuation = true -- SFSpeechRecognizer returns one
  unbroken run-on phrase with no punctuation at all otherwise.
- Fixed a crash ("Range requires lowerBound <= upperBound") in
  ReflectionSessionStore.updateLiveTranscript's pause-gap-detection loop,
  triggered by a lengthy reflection crossing a segment rollover.
- Raised SpeakerDiarizationEngine's minimumSpeakerDuration/Proportion
  floors (3.0s/0.15 -> 8.0s/0.25) after a solo monologue was relabeled as
  three speakers -- flagged in STABILIZATION_AND_EXPRESSION_PLAN.md as an
  anecdote-driven stopgap needing a real fix, not a permanent tuning.
- Commissioned and incorporated an external (ChatGPT-assisted) review of
  the speech pipeline against three real test recordings, which confirmed
  deeper defects (word timing, lifecycle races, diarization matching) not
  visible from symptom reports alone. See
  STABILIZATION_AND_EXPRESSION_PLAN.md.
- Clarified the product's actual purpose: analyzing *how* speech is
  expressed (prolongation, rate, articulation, pitch/intensity), not just
  transcript content -- promoting expressive-speech analysis from an
  explicitly-deferred idea to a core, staged workstream.
- Added ONBOARDING.md and STABILIZATION_AND_EXPRESSION_PLAN.md.

Known issues documented, fix plan written, implementation not yet started

Closes point F of Stage 0 (the test-only replay harness validating the
saved fixtures through the corrected recognition/timing path) and runs a
ten-experiment investigation (E1-E10) into why on-device transcription
fidelity failed the 70% floor on all three fixtures. Not a single
feature; summary:

- Added Observatory/SpeechTranscriptTimingMapper.swift, extracting the
  segment-to-utterance mapping shared by both the live mic path and the
  new replay harness.
- Rebuilt ObservatoryTests/FixtureReplayHarness.swift and added
  ObservatoryTests/AudioFixtureReplayTests.swift: single-request replay,
  chunked replay matching production's ~1-minute rollover, a real
  AVAudioEngine/AVAudioPlayerNode live-tap feed path, a raw per-callback
  instrumentation hook, and a silence-padded passage-position control --
  all numeric-only output, never transcript text (SECURITY.md).
- Models.swift additions supporting the above: audioRecordingReliable,
  missingAudioIntervals, RawDiarizationSegment, DiarizationFilteringDecision,
  optional word audioStart/audioEnd.
- Generated new, gitignored fixture variants for controlled A/B testing
  (loudnorm, identity re-encode, fixed per-fixture gain) -- the three
  original fixtures stay read-only baselines, untouched, and (like all
  fixtures) are not tracked in git.
- Found and fixed a Swift 6 sendability warning (a plain captured `var`
  read from a non-isolated AsyncThrowingStream termination closure) by
  wrapping the feeding Task in the same lock-protected box pattern already
  used elsewhere in the harness.
- Root-caused the fidelity floor failure (E8/E9, on physical iPhone 16 Pro

  replacement-not-cumulative semantics across internal utterance

  regardless of position when the padding is silence, not competing

  helps two of three fixtures partially, not the third at all), feed
  mechanism (manual-append loop vs. a real engine tap), re-encoding, and

  across two review passes (PROPOSALS/U1-chatgpt-review-brief*.md) -- the

  universal API rule.
- Reframed the U2 privacy/fidelity decision: prior fidelity numbers
  undercount what the local recognizer actually produced during
  processing (final-result-only collection discards it), so they don't
  yet establish the local recognizer's achievable accuracy. Queued a new
  unit (U5) to prototype transcript accumulation across reset events,
  test-only first, with required accuracy/timing/duplicate-detection
  validation before any production change -- a naive drop-and-concatenate
  approach is explicitly not approved as a production algorithm.

v119 — U6: interruption-chain recovery, bounded failure budget, honest capture-completeness record

Date: 2026-10-23

Fixes the two production capture-lifecycle defects the live smoke test exposed in v118: recognition could get permanently stuck after a chained interruption (Siri -> Maps -> return) with no way back, and the reliability record stayed "clean" even when real audio went uncaptured. Review history: ChatGPT brief #3 (pre-implementation, approve-with-changes) drove proposal revisions R1-R2.5; the director's code inspection of the first implementation caught a watchdog-gate defect (R3); the first device run's failure drove R4 (recovery fixes) and ChatGPT brief #4 ruled on the capture-completeness schema (R5, -> D-17). Two device smoke-test runs, first FAILED, second PASSED with documented caveats.

- SpeechRecognitionManager.swift: replaced the two independent isRecording/
  wasRecordingBeforeInterruption booleans with an explicit lifecycle state
  machine (idle/active/recovering/stopped) plus a per-reflection
  reflectionID that invalidates stale triggers. One shared
  attemptRecovery(trigger:forReflection:) path now serves routine
  rollover, recognition errors, interruption-ended resume, a new 20s
  watchdog, and a new scenePhase-foreground-reconciliation backstop --
  gated by two independent stall conditions (Mode A: recognition silent
  while capture continues; Mode B: stuck in .recovering after a failed
  restart, found by the first device run and fixed by removing the
  Mode-A-only buffers-flowing requirement that made a dead engine
  permanently unrecoverable). A new consecutive-failure budget (separate
  from the existing rate-window guard, which stays unchanged) ends a
  reflection honestly after five failed restart attempts instead of
  silently stopping forever.
- A new AudioWriterBox (mirroring the existing CurrentRequestBox/
  GenerationBox pattern) fixes a stale-tap-reference bug: the realtime
  tap's buffer callback now reads the current writer through a
  lock-protected box instead of a frozen capture-list snapshot, so a
  discarded writer can never keep silently receiving buffers.
  discardCurrentAudioWriterIfAny() flushes, then deletes a writer with
  zero successfully-written frames (verified by frame count, not file
  size) or keeps one with real content.
- Models.swift: new ReflectionSession.recognitionContinuityGaps ([Date]?,
  wall-clock instants only, no fabricated duration) for recognition-only
  gaps while capture continues, and new captureCompleteness
  (CaptureCompleteness?: .complete / .incomplete(endpointAudioSeconds:) /
  nil=unknown-legacy) for whole-session capture completeness, established
  only from lifecycle evidence (an interruption's .began teardown),
  never erased by a later successful recovery, never from a duration/
  file-length mismatch alone (D-17). audioRecordingReliable/
  missingAudioIntervals keep their exact prior write-integrity meaning,
  unchanged.
- SpeakerDiarizationEngine.swift: skips diarization entirely on a
  confirmed-incomplete session, alongside the existing unreliable-audio
  skip.
- ReflectionSessionStore.swift: new handleRecordingEndedUnexpectedly(
  reflectionID:reason:finishAudioCapture:), wired from both screens, so a
  reflection that ends via the new failure budget (not an explicit user
  Stop) still finalizes honestly instead of staying stuck in .recording
  forever.
- Fixed a bug found while building the above: finishWithFailure() was
  discarding the audio writer before the callback chain ever reached
  finishAudioCapture(), so every terminal-failure session -- exactly the
  ones this unit exists to diagnose -- silently lost its reliability/
  completeness diagnostics. It now only discards eagerly when no listener
  is wired; otherwise finishAudioCapture() is the single place that reads
  diagnostics and discards, for both a normal Stop and a terminal
  failure.
- [u6-recovery]-prefixed diagnostic logging added throughout the recovery
  path (trigger/state/generation on every attempt, the actual error
  domain/code on a failed restart, interruption .began/.ended details,
  watchdog scheduling tokens) -- counts/identifiers/timings only, no
  transcript text, per SECURITY.md.
- ObservatoryTests/ObservatoryTests.swift: added logic-level simulator
  tests covering the recovery state machine, both stall modes, the
  zero/partial-write writer-discard decision, and captureCompleteness's
  five acceptance scenarios (normal stop, unrecovered outage, outage then
  recovery, delayed writer draining, legacy decode) -- 53/53 simulator-
  eligible tests pass (the project's 8 pre-existing device-only
  AudioFixtureReplayTests diagnostics fail in a bare simulator host for
  an unrelated, pre-existing reason: no granted Speech-recognition
  authorization).
- Two live on-device smoke-test runs, both Hermes-forensically verified
  against device-container backups and console logs. First run (iPhone
  16 Pro) FAILED: found the Mode-B zombie-recovery defect and the
  dishonest reliability record, plus a minor duplicate-watchdog-firing
  anomaly under investigation. Second run PASSED with documented
  caveats: the Mode-B watchdog retry succeeded this time after an
  initial restart failure (NSOSStatusErrorDomain 561015905, "Session
  activation failed" -- now logged), 69 words transcribed after
  recovery, and the session record was honest (captureCompleteness=
  .incomplete with the exact file endpoint, diarization skipped, both
  recognitionContinuityGaps timestamps inside the actual outage window).

Status:

U6 implemented and device-verified with caveats (see PLAN.md for the
full criterion-by-criterion record of both runs). Caveats carried

v120 — U6b: fast-backoff restart retry

Date: 2026-10-24

Shrinks the post-interruption dead window v119 (U6) left open: after a failed restart, recovery previously waited for the next fixed 20-second watchdog tick (observed ~36s/~46s dead windows in U6's two device runs). Mini-unit, director-reviewed directly (no ChatGPT round -- bounded extension of the already-reviewed U6 recovery architecture); one review cycle (Revision 1) required two fixes before implementation, both based on the director's own timeline math, not a new finding from a device run.

- SpeechRecognitionManager.swift: a new RecoveryTrigger case,
  .scheduledFastRetry, lets a failed attemptRecovery() call schedule its
  own bounded follow-up (2s/4s/8s/16s backoff, fastRetryBackoffSeconds)
  instead of only waiting for the next trigger. Exactly one fast retry
  can be pending at a time (pendingFastRetryTask); any attempt that
  actually proceeds -- a fresh fast retry, the watchdog, foreground
  reconciliation, or an interruption's .ended -- cancels whatever was
  previously pending first, so the fast-retry chain and the watchdog's
  independent 20s cadence can never both execute for the same failure.
  Once the 4-entry backoff schedule is exhausted, scheduling silently
  stops and the watchdog's own cadence carries the rest of the budget --
  this is intentional, not a bug (see the next bullet).
- Fast retries count toward the SAME consecutive-failure budget as every
  other trigger -- no second counter. Because of that,
  maxConsecutiveRecoveryFailures was raised from 5 to 10: at 5, a
  continuously-failing restart would have exhausted the budget by the
  fast schedule's own ~30-second mark, ending a reflection SOONER than
  v119's watchdog-only design ever would have (whose 5-strikes-at-20s
  budget gave ~100s, inside which the v119 passing run actually
  recovered). 10 keeps the worst-case floor at or above that ~100s
  baseline (traced timeline: fast retries exhaust the budget's first 4-5
  slots by ~20-30s, the remaining 5 slots can only be filled by the
  watchdog's independent 20s-apart ticks, landing exhaustion at roughly
  120-130s) while still letting the common case -- the interrupting
  app releasing the session within the first few seconds -- recover far
  sooner than before.
- A fresh interruption beginning while a fast retry is pending must not
  let it fire into the OS still owning the microphone (it would fail
  deterministically and burn a budget step for nothing, something no
  other v119 trigger does). Two layers: handleInterruption(.began) now
  cancels any pending fast retry directly, and attemptRecovery()
  independently refuses a .scheduledFastRetry trigger while
  interruptionInProgress is true, closing the race between those two
  separately-scheduled async hops regardless of which lands first.
  handleInterruption(type:optionsValue:) changed from private to
  internal (access-level only, no behavior change) so a test can drive
  a simulated .began directly.
- [u6-recovery] logging extended to cover fast-retry scheduling, firing,
  and cancellation, matching the existing trigger/state/generation
  logging style -- counts/identifiers/timings only, no transcript text.
- ObservatoryTests/ObservatoryTests.swift: nine new logic-level
  simulator tests covering scheduling under budget, budget exhaustion,
  an actual end-to-end backoff firing (one real ~2.5s wait, the rest
  deterministic), Stop/new-reflection/racing-trigger cancellation, the
  rate-guard margin at the schedule's densest cluster, schedule
  exhaustion not being confused with budget exhaustion, and the
  interruption-vs-pending-retry race -- plus a cleanup fix to one
  pre-existing test so it no longer leaves a stray fast-retry Task
  running past its own completion. 62/62 simulator-eligible tests pass
  (same 8 pre-existing environmental AudioFixtureReplayTests failures as
  every prior unit, unrelated to this work).
- One live on-device smoke-test run (iPhone, same scripted-phrase repro
  as U6's), Hermes-forensically verified against the device-container
  backup and console log. PASSED. The .ended restart failed again
  (NSOSStatusErrorDomain 561015905, the same "Session activation failed"
  as U6); a 2-second fast retry fired and also failed; a 4-second retry
  was scheduled and then never fired because iOS suspended the
  backgrounded app, freezing the retry chain entirely -- on the user's
  return, the watchdog fired immediately, visibly cancelled the stale

2026-10-24

The first transcript-independent acoustic measurements in this project,
computed directly on saved .caf recordings via AVAudioFile + Accelerate
with no recognizer involved at any point. Test-only: 11 new files under ObservatoryTests/
(2 more than the proposal listed: window aggregation and test support got
their own files), no production-target file touched. No pbxproj edit was
needed (the test target is a fileSystemSynchronizedGroup, so the approved
Q5a edit turned out unnecessary); the only file touched outside
ObservatoryTests/ is Observatory.xctestplan, which sets
OBSERVATORY_U4A_SESSION_BACKUP_DIR so the real-session tests actually run.

Scope was set by a ChatGPT review (APPROVE WITH CHANGES) and the
director's split of U4a into U4a-1 (Channel A, simulator, milestones
M1-M4) and U4a-2 (Channel B / SFVoiceAnalytics, device, M5 -- not started).
SFVoiceAnalytics is reachable only through recognition results, so it
cannot satisfy transcript-independence; whether its frame arrays can be
anchored to file time at all is an open question U4a-2 will answer, and
no claim is made here about how much audio it covers.

What the measurement layer does: a four-state frame classification that
keeps speech activity, periodic voicing, pause candidates and unknown
regions separate (unvoiced speech is not a pause -- conflating them, as
the original design did, corrupted the pause, phonation and articulation-
rate definitions); NCCF fundamental frequency in Hz with sub-harmonic
preference, out-of-range peaks reported as out-of-range rather than as
unvoiced, and octave-jump rejection that nils a frame instead of
substituting a value; dBFS level; a span-scoped percentile noise floor; a
floor-relative level proxy that is explicitly NOT called SNR; detected-
nucleus events and rates that are explicitly NOT called syllables per
second. Window aggregation carries metric-specific coverage (domain,
eligible, estimation-success, absolute seconds) and a validity record with
one primary reason plus non-exclusive quality flags. Thresholds are
SignalLibrary-shaped rule data; estimator parameters are a separate
versioned, SHA-256-hashed configuration recorded inline in every report.
Neither is a Swift constant (D-16).

Simulator evidence: 85 tests, 77 passed, 8 failed -- all 15 new tests pass;
the 8 failures are the pre-existing speech-permission replay failures,
unchanged. M1 (estimator checks against known answers): F0 error <= 0.001%
at 100/150/220/300 Hz and at both 60/400 Hz search bounds; missing-
fundamental and abrupt-150-to-300 Hz cases exact; level error 0.001 dB;
nucleus counts exact (5/5, 10/10); activity boundaries 0.995/2.005 s
against truth 1.000/2.000; a level-matched noise burst yields 0 voiced
frames of 90, confirming activity and voicing are not conflated; floor
error -0.14 dB. F0 abstains entirely at 5 dB SNR (0 eligible frames) --
reported, not hidden. M4 (invariance on existing assets): F0 identical to
0.000% across base/identity/gain variants of all three fixtures, and
measured level change exactly +12.00/+15.20/+18.70 dB against the applied
gains recorded in E10b.

M2, the behavioural milestone -- real measurements from real recordings,
with the artifact as evidence: U4aMeasurementReport.json, 10.3 MB, attached
to the test run and exported from the xcresult bundle; 5 real sources,
6,926 window measurements, 1,340 explicit nulls, 5,586 valid, and 1 real
rule-fired observation (fixture-solo, intensity fall, -6.42 dB against an
uncalibrated -6.0 dB threshold). All three fixtures and the U6b session
reached the full-demonstration outcome with every pre-registered
plausibility band in band. The U6b incomplete session was analysed
coverage-scoped per D-18: 95.9 s analyzable of 149.4 s captured across 7
spans, with the lifecycle-derived join bound reported as the INTERVAL
[13.40, 32.40] s -- not the single 32.4 s point the first draft claimed,
which the review correctly identified as algebra rather than corroboration
-- kept separate from 7 near-silence and 2 noise-floor-step signal
candidates. A pre-D-17 session with no captureCompleteness field decoded
as unknown-legacy and abstained from all 360 join-sensitive measurements
while still reporting 72 frame-local level values.

M3 (abstention and schema): zero observations from digital silence, from
-50 dBFS white noise, and from the real 4096-byte header-only orphaned
recording (handled, no crash); a detectable join bounded to 4.01-5.99 s
against a 4.0-6.0 s truth and split into two usable spans; a seamless join
correctly detected by nothing at all, which is why waveform inspection
cannot replace lifecycle evidence; schema scan clean (0 violations, 49
explicit nulls, 84 legitimate zeros); byte-identical canonical payload
across runs within this pinned environment.

Three defects were found by these checks and fixed before any green run:
NCCF sub-harmonic selection (a 300 Hz signal read as 100 Hz and a 400 Hz
signal as 100 Hz); inconsistent rate denominators producing an impossible
estimation-success value of 201.0; and the analysis-domain builder
deleting an entire span when a join's location was unbounded, where the
specification says frame-local measurements survive. Four pre-registration
amendments (A1-A4) are documented with their reasons inside the JSON files
they affect.

Explicitly not claimed: nothing here is perceptually, emotionally or
clinically validated, and no interpretation, emotion label, probability or
severity is produced anywhere. M1 and M4 establish synthetic accuracy and
gain invariance only. The three fixtures' estimated noise floor sits at
-84 to -87 dBFS because they contain near-digital-silence stretches, so
the floor-relative proxy is relative to that rather than to room tone;
every real-source measurement carries a noiseFloorUnverifiedOnRealAudio
flag, because real audio offers no independent floor measurement to check
it against. Nucleus rates are reference-unscored estimates. Channel B,
speaker attribution, production integration and all of Stage 2 remain
untouched.
