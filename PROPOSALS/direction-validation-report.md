## Executive summary (one page, plain language)

**The core idea survives the attack — with two honest limits.**

Think of Observatory as building a **thermometer, not a diagnosis**. The scientific literature says a well-built thermometer for the voice is real: pitch, pauses, speed, and voice-quality measures genuinely move with mental state, and among them **pauses and timing have the strongest evidence** (in schizophrenia research, pause measures show some of the largest effects ever recorded in voice; in depression studies, aggregation over weeks measurably strengthens weak single-sample signals). What the literature just as clearly says: nobody — not academia, not well-funded companies — can yet honestly claim "this voice measurement means this mood." A 2026 meta-analysis could pool only 6 studies well enough to test the classic pitch claim, and the result was statistically **insignificant**. Published AI accuracies collapse when tested properly on new people (97.6% → 58.7% in one controlled re-test). Your own architecture (D-02/D-03: observe expression, never assert emotion) already sits on the right side of this line. The study's verdict: **the observation instrument is scientifically defensible today; the mood-inference product is not, and won't be without reference annotation you don't have yet.**

**The FDA plan is discipline, not destiny.** No voice-biomarker mental-health device has ever received FDA marketing authorization. Kintsugi — the company that tried hardest, with 14,000 participants, a peer-reviewed pivotal-quality study, four years of FDA pre-submission meetings, and $30M — **shut down in early 2026 with its application still pending**, and open-sourced its model. The companies that got FDA clearance in digital therapeutics at all (Pear, Akili) died commercially anyway, killed by insurance reimbursement, not by the FDA. The good news: "build to SaMD quality now" costs *this* project almost nothing, because the habits you already enforce (written decisions, frozen thresholds, honest failure records, measurements that carry their own coverage caveats) are exactly what regulators ask for — and a 2025 FDA framework (PCCP) specifically rewards your "signal libraries as versioned data" design. The verdict: **keep the quality habits, defer the formal certification bureaucracy until a concrete trigger** (first disease claim, first enterprise customer, first funded study). Do not let "FDA later" drive today's scope.

**Apple broke the transcript layer — and the project hedged correctly, but on slightly stale facts.** The verified E8 defect (transcripts losing most of their content after pauses) is not the project's bug: at least six independent developers report the same thing, including in **Apple's own sample app**, with five formal bug filings. Your hedge — own the acoustic measurement path (Channel A), treat Apple's analytics as measured-not-relied-on (Channel B) — is the right call, and your own worst incident proved the premise: when the transcript died, the audio survived. But the study found a **fact that changes the picture**: Apple's new on-device speech engine (SpeechAnalyzer, iOS 26+) is present in the Xcode installed on this machine — the earlier "absent from SDK" premise is stale — and independent benchmarks show it roughly **halving error rates** versus the old engine on real long audio, with proper word timings. Before investing in U5's complex workaround machinery, the project should spend one small unit re-testing E8 on the current OS and evaluating SpeechAnalyzer.

**Your process is the project's quiet superpower.** The three-agent split, one-unit-one-fresh-chat, file-first memory, and artifact verification match, point for point, the mitigations that the 2024–2026 literature on AI-coding failures recommends — and it has already caught four real failures in the act (a flattering "fixed" handoff that wasn't, non-reproducible agent tallies, code written without a compiler, contaminated test runs). The measurement disciplines (frozen pre-registration, never-0-always-nil, abstention-over-fabrication) exceed what most academic voice papers do. What's missing is not discipline but **evidence**: no calibration against real ground truth, no repeat-measurement reliability on real speakers, one device, three fixtures.

**Bottom line:** continue; adjust two things. (1) Insert a cheap ASR-platform decision point before U5. (2) Start buying measurement validity cheaply — re-record fixtures under current settings, cross-validate the F0 estimator against Praat/YIN on public corpora, and begin test–retest on a real speaker. None of this requires re-sequencing the vision; it protects it.

## Domain 1 — Scientific validity of the core idea

**Question:** can voice-acoustic measures (F0, jitter, shimmer, intensity, pause/silence, speech/articulation rate) honestly support "measurement-grade observation of human behavior and mood"?

### Strongest case FOR

1. **[Measured]** Group-level acoustic differences in depression are repeatedly reported: pitch/loudness variables separated MDD patients from controls with large effects (η² 0.183–0.30), shimmer separated moderate from mild depression, pause behavior shifted with severity (BMC Psychiatry 2024, doi:10.1186/s12888-024-06253-6). A 2023 Frontiers in Psychiatry model correlated r = 0.684 with HAMD severity.
2. **[Measured]** The timing family — precisely what U4a extracts (pauses, rate, phonation time) — carries the strongest cross-diagnostic evidence: a Bayesian meta-analysis of voice in schizophrenia (46 articles, 1,254 patients) found Hedges' g from −0.75 to **−1.89** for proportion-of-spoken-time, speech rate, and pause duration, versus only −0.55 for pitch variability.
3. **[Measured]** Longitudinal aggregation works: Sonde Health's prospective cohort study (n=104 psychiatric outpatients) showed risk ratios for elevated symptom severity improving from 1.53 (single 30-s samples) to **2.00** (two-week aggregates) using eight acoustic features of the same family Observatory measures (jitter, shimmer, pitch variability, energy variability, phonation duration, speech rate, pause duration). This directly supports Observatory's longitudinal, session-accumulating design.
4. **[Measured]** Real-world commercial screening reaches modest-but-real discrimination: Ellipsis-type acoustic+semantic screening at AUC 0.79–0.83 on 2,086 real care-management calls (JMIR AI 2025); Kintsugi at 71.3% sensitivity / 73.5% specificity on >14,000 participants (Annals of Family Medicine 2025).
5. **[Inference]** The physiological rationale is not mystical: depression's psychomotor retardation and arousal changes plausibly alter voice production (the foundational Cummins et al. 2015 review, Speech Communication 71:10–49). And the field's own skeptics say the problem is overclaiming, not absence of signal — which is the exact problem Observatory's honesty machinery (D-03, D-18, D-19) is built to avoid.

### Strongest case AGAINST

1. **[Measured]** The most recent quantitative synthesis is discouraging: from 31 publications, only 6 had extractable F0 data; the single poolable estimate — mean F0 difference, depressed vs controls — was **−1.82 Hz, not statistically significant** (p = 0.56). Jitter, shimmer, HNR, and other parameters **could not be pooled at all** due to inconsistent reporting (Cantor-Cutiva et al. 2026, Depression and Anxiety, doi:10.1155/da/5592230).
2. **[Measured]** Small matched studies contradict the big ones: n=30 vs 30 found **no significant group differences** in jitter, shimmer, HNR, F0 or SD-F0 (Depression Research and Treatment 2023).
3. **[Measured]** Generalization failures are documented with hard numbers: speaker-leakage-controlled re-splits drop accuracy 97.65% → 58.74% (arXiv 2604.14354); a leakage-controlled acoustic model trained on prenatal depression transferred at **AUC 0.505/0.478 — chance** — to DAIC-WOZ/E-DAIC (PMC13622871); on extended DAIC-WOZ, **shuffling the labels did not significantly degrade** most systems' performance (arXiv 2606.31730); WinterLight's published Alzheimer's detection "only worked for English speakers of a particular Canadian dialect" (co-founder, via Quartz).
4. **[Measured]** The benchmark datasets Observatory would need to validate against are weak matches for its real input: IEMOCAP is 10 actors; RAVDESS/CREMA-D are acted; DEAP (32 participants) and MAHNOB-HCI (27) are tiny, lab-elicited, WEIRD samples where emotion is induced by media rather than expressed in spontaneous speech; DAIC-WOZ is mostly US veterans with an imbalanced 107/35 split, and has never been applied to external data ("Method Matters," PMC12122156). None matches single-speaker iPhone-mic self-directed reflections at −37 to −46 dBFS.
5. **[Measured]** The bespoke NCCF estimator is a legitimate four-decades-old family (core of Talkin's RAPT) but Observatory's implementation is not RAPT (no dynamic-programming post-processing), and plain NCCF is known for octave errors. Without a cross-estimator agreement study (same audio through Praat/YIN/eGeMAPS), Observatory's F0 — and the jitter/shimmer that condition on pitch periods — are **literature-incomparable**: internally consistent, externally unanchored. The field's standard comparability set is GeMAPS/eGeMAPS (Eyben et al. 2015/2016, IEEE T-AFFC), whose stated purpose is exactly to "eliminate differences caused by… different implementations of the same parameters."

### Grade: **VALIDATED-WITH-RESERVATIONS** — for observation; **INVALIDATED** for inference

Defensible today, with zero annotation: descriptive acoustic measurement with provenance and coverage ("this session's median F0 was X, covering Y% of eligible frames"); within-person longitudinal change as *a signal worth noticing*, never a diagnosis; pause/timing/rate claims (strongest literature); honest-uncertainty instrumentation as the differentiator (the field's failure record is a record of overclaiming).

Not defensible today: any mood/emotion/depression inference; any claim that Observatory's F0 equals literature F0 (needs the cross-estimator study); any device/condition generality (n=1 device, 3 fixtures); any clinical claim of any class.

**What would change the grade:** (a) a cross-estimator agreement study against Praat/YIN/eGeMAPS on public corpora plus the project's own fixtures — cheap, no mood annotation needed — would move the measurement layer toward full validation; (b) test–retest data on real speakers (ICC/MDC); (c) any move toward inference claims requires reference annotation and a validation study against an accepted anchor — and the literature says even then, transportability across populations is the open scientific question of the field.

## Domain 2 — SaMD/FDA trajectory realism

**Question:** what does FDA actually require for a behavioral-measurement claim; can a personal single-user app with no clinical workflow plausibly get there; is "built to SaMD quality now, submission later" sound or premature?

### What FDA actually requires (measured, from retrieved guidance)

- **Whether it's a device at all** turns on intended use. FDA's Device Software Functions guidance (final, Sep 2022) puts "log/record/track/evaluate behavior related to general fitness, health or wellness" in the **not-a-device** category, and coping-skill/coaching software for people with diagnosed psychiatric conditions under **enforcement discretion**. The General Wellness guidance (final, **Jan 6, 2026**) explicitly lists relaxation/stress management, mental acuity, and self-esteem as low-risk wellness claims — and explicitly excludes "helps treat an anxiety disorder"-style claims. Observatory's Phase 1 as written (bounded reflections, describe/suggest, no diagnosis — constraints §6) sits in the safe zone **as long as product language never references a disease**. The moment the claims ladder reaches "predict/influence" tied to ADHD/alexithymia as conditions, it is a device needing a submission. This confirms VISION §5's gates-not-dates doctrine: each rung of the ladder is a regulatory event.
- **A cleared measurement claim** requires, at minimum (per Kintsugi's own FDA-hosted DHAC slides, Nov 2025 — the best public window into what FDA demanded of exactly this device class): a locked intended use; a **prospective, double-blind pivotal study** against an accepted reference standard (they used SCID-5 clinician interviews); pre-agreed performance lower bounds (75/75 sensitivity/specificity, 95% CI); human-factors evidence; cybersecurity and software-documentation packages; and years of pre-submission engagement (4 pre-subs over 4 years inside a 7-year journey). Kintsugi also candidly reported that its gold standard itself is noisy (clinician agreement κ = 0.28). For alexithymia — Observatory's beachhead — there is **no SCID equivalent**, so the reference-standard problem is plausibly harder, not easier.
- **Quality system:** 21 CFR 820 became the QMSR (effective **Feb 2, 2026**), incorporating **ISO 13485:2016 by reference** — one QMS now serves FDA, EU, and enterprise customers simultaneously. The expected standards stack: IEC 62304 (software lifecycle), ISO 14971 (risk), IEC 62366 (usability).
- **PCCP (final, Aug 18, 2025):** FDA's Predetermined Change Control Plan framework lets pre-authorized model/threshold changes ship without a new submission. Observatory's "signal libraries as versioned data, not code" (D-16, constraints §7) with acceptance criteria is *literally the shape* of a PCCP modification protocol practiced early. This is the single most Observatory-friendly regulatory development of the last two years.

### Precedents and outcomes (measured)

- **[Absent-evidence finding, bounded search]** As of 2026-10-24, **no voice-biomarker mental-health device holds FDA marketing authorization** — no 510(k), no De Novo. No FDA enforcement action against a voice-biomarker consumer app was found either.
- **Kintsugi**: the closest real attempt. >14,000 participants, peer-reviewed (Ann Fam Med 2025, Sn 71.3%/Sp 73.5%), prospective double-blind pivotal vs SCID-5, $30M, 7 years — **shut down Feb–Apr 2026 with the De Novo pending**; open-sourced its model. Its CEO's post-mortem: years spent "teaching the regulator about AI"; the De Novo framework fits continuously-updated models poorly. (The Verge, Apr 2, 2026 — director-verified.)
- **Ellipsis Health**: alive; pivoted to an "emotionally intelligent AI care manager" ($45M, Jun 2025); no clearance. **Sonde Health**: alive as an un-cleared B2B API + wellness-positioned consumer tracker. **WinterLight**: acquired by Cambridge Cognition (~$8.6M) after its dialect-generalization failure; its actual business became clinical-trial endpoint tooling (no device clearance needed for research use). **Canary Speech**: sells un-cleared analytics with enterprise certifications (ISO 27001/42001, HITRUST); a reported Breakthrough designation could not be verified on primary sources. **Mindstrong** (digital biomarkers): shut down 2023 after ~$160M.
- **The cleared DTx graveyard**: Pear (reSET K162510, Somryst K191716) — Chapter 11 in 2023, sold for parts (~$6M). Akili (EndeavorRx De Novo DEN200026, which created 21 CFR 882.5803, Class II QFT) — FY2023 revenue $1.7M, sold at $0.434/share in 2025. Woebot — Breakthrough designation 2021, never cleared, app shut down June 2025. **Clearance ≠ commercial success; reimbursement killed the winners.**
- **EU**: AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) (in force since Feb 2, 2025) **prohibits** emotion inference in workplaces and education — and call centers are workplaces. This is a near-fatal wall for Track II's call-center concept in the EU; VISION §3's flag is confirmed, and the Commission's own guidelines cite "serious concerns about the scientific basis" of emotion inference (limited reliability, lack of specificity, limited generalisability). MDR Rule 11 makes any decision-support claim **Class IIa minimum** (notified body; no self-certification), and the EU has no wellness safe harbor as generous as FDA's.

### Strongest case FOR "SaMD quality now, submit later"

1. **[Measured]** For this specific codebase the marginal cost is near zero: the three-agent protocol is already producing design-history-grade evidence (proposals = design inputs; reviews = design review; artifact verification = design verification). VISION §6's "documentation-mapping exercise, not a rebuild" is credible here in a way it wouldn't be for a typical startup.
2. **[Measured]** QMSR/ISO 13485 harmonization removed the "two quality systems" penalty; PCCP rewards the versioned-library architecture already mandated.
3. **[Measured]** Retrofit is genuinely expensive in one direction: provenance and measurement-validity architecture cannot be retrofitted cheaply, and no QMS can repair missing ground truth. Practitioner evidence agrees late QMS is a rebuild (Veranex 2026; MSI cost analysis: cheapest routes "produce the highest total cost over three years").
4. **[Measured]** FDA is exacting, not hostile: 1,200+ AI-enabled devices authorized, a standing Digital Health Advisory Committee, structured pre-sub process Kintsugi itself called "thoughtful and rigorous."

### Strongest case AGAINST

1. **[Measured]** The binding constraint is **clinical evidence, not code quality**. A pivotal study needs real patients, reference-standard administration, sites, and multi-year funding. Observatory has one user. No amount of IEC 62304 conformance bends that curve.
2. **[Measured]** The destination's market value keeps getting marked down: the two companies that achieved clearance died anyway; the one that pursued voice-biomarker clearance hardest died before crossing the line. Spending SaMD-grade effort against that endpoint is spending against a lottery ticket.
3. **[Inference]** The single-user, no-clinical-workflow form factor is a validation *liability*, not a simplification: no supervised acoustics, no reference-standard administration, self-selected users, no clinician to absorb uncertainty. A personal self-measurement claim is the **least precedented** claim in the space.
4. **[Inference]** Over-engineering risk is asymmetric: formal ISO certification ($5k+ minimum), full 62304/14971/62366 artifact sets, and controlled-change overhead would multiply the cost of every unit of work for a fast-iterating personal app whose regulatory decision may never come. The consultant advice to "start with your quality system" is calibrated for venture-backed device companies with investors to satisfy.

### Grade: **VALIDATED-WITH-RESERVATIONS** (B−)

A− as engineering discipline; C as a business/regulatory plan. The split verdict the evidence supports: **build the epistemics of SaMD now** (provenance, uncertainty, traceability, honest claims, PCCP-shaped libraries — already mandated, already cheap here); **defer the bureaucracy** (formal ISO 13485 certification, full 62304 documentation classes, DHF formality) until a concrete trigger: first disease-referencing claim, first Track II customer with compliance requirements, or first funded validation program. Apply the project's own gates-not-dates doctrine to QMS formalization itself.

**What would change the grade:** a funded validation partner or clinical site network (upward, toward validated); any product language drifting into disease-referencing claims before a submission (downward — that is unmarketed-device risk, and it's a wording discipline the claims ladder already encodes); evidence of any voice-biomarker clearance anywhere (would materially de-risk the trajectory).

## Domain 3 — Platform risk (Apple speech stack and the two-channel hedge)

**Question:** given verified E8, deprecated segment-level voiceAnalytics, and iOS-26-era churn, is "own DSP primary, Apple analytics measured-not-relied-on" the right hedge within D-04 (on-device only)?

### E8 is corroborated — and scoped correctly (measured)

- Apple developer forum thread 762952 (retrieved in full; 40 replies, 16 participants): ≥6 independent reports of pause-triggered transcript loss, **reproducing in Apple's own SpokenWord sample app** — zero custom code. Five Feedback Assistant filings shared in-thread; Apple DTS engaged, no public fix commitment. On iOS 17 the defect was on-device-only; on iOS 18 it hit both server and on-device flags. Two more threads (731761, 770278) report the same class.
- The project's own E8 audit logged drops in raw callbacks **before any Observatory mapping code**, and Apple's sample app shows the same symptom — two disjoint codebases, one of them Apple's. "It's their own lifecycle bug" is effectively excluded. PLAN.md's scoped language ("on THIS device/OS/request configuration — not established as a universal API rule") is exactly right, because:
- **An unresolved tension:** the thread's accepted answer (Oct 2025) claims the bug was **fixed in iOS 26 RC** — yet E8/E9 reproduced on the project's iOS-27-era device in Oct 2026. Possible reconciliations (speculative, in order of plausibility): the fix landed unevenly across on-device vs server paths; the fixer's test didn't exercise on-device-required long-form requests; a regression; or what Observatory sees is a related-but-distinct behavior. **No source settles it.** This makes a cheap isolated E8 re-run on the current OS build worth doing before sizing U5.
- **Apple has never documented cumulative-final semantics.** The current docs say a result contains "transcriptions of *the current utterance*" — wording that arguably *supports* replacement-per-utterance readings. Nobody should plan around either the bug or its fix; both are undocumented behavior.
- The production corroboration is the project's own smoke-test forensics: **audio survived a chained Siri/Maps interruption while the transcript died** — the core premise of Channel A (audio outlives Apple's recognition output), demonstrated by the project's worst incident.

### API state (measured; one premise corrected)

- `SFVoiceAnalytics` (the class) is **not deprecated**; the *segment-level* `SFTranscriptionSegment.voiceAnalytics` was deprecated in **iOS 14.5**, with the supported path being result-level `SFSpeechRecognitionMetadata.voiceAnalytics` (current). U4a's Channel-B design matches Apple's actual API topology. Caveat: nothing documents that metadata coverage spans the whole request — under E8 semantics it may be utterance-scoped, which is precisely what M5 exists to measure (any coverage value passes, including near-zero).
- **SpeechAnalyzer/SpeechTranscriber ARE present in the installed Xcode 27.0 SDK** (director-verified directly: `Speech.swiftmodule/arm64e-apple-ios.swiftinterface`, `final public actor SpeechAnalyzer` line 228, `final public class SpeechTranscriber` line 346, word-level `.audioTimeRange` result attribute line 394; also `DictationTranscriber` for older devices and `SpeechDetector` VAD). iOS 26.0+, fully on-device (D-04-compliant), purpose-built for long-form audio and conversation, powers Notes/Voice Memos. **The "absent from the installed SDK" premise in DECISIONS.md's out-of-scope ruling is stale.** One property in the interface already carries a `deprecated: 27` marker — churn is active even inside the replacement API.
- **Independent benchmarks:** SpeechAnalyzer 10.3% WER vs SFSpeechRecognizer 23.8% on a real 30-minute multi-voice pipeline, with deletions 1.4% vs 6.8% and sentence-end recall 89.8% vs 43.8% (macdub, Sep 2026); 2.12% vs 9.02% on clean LibriSpeech (Inscribe, Jul 2026 — vendor benchmark, read speech, directionally useful). SFSpeechRecognizer's own XPC service measured *heavier* CPU (27.6% vs 5.7% avg). `SFSpeechRecognizer` itself is not formally deprecated — Apple's replacement is rhetorical and practical, not announced.
- On-device third-party fallbacks within D-04 exist and are viable for batch-from-file transcription (Observatory's actual pattern — recordings are saved first): whisper.cpp/WhisperKit (MIT, ~40–460 MB models, ANE-accelerated; accuracy below SpeechAnalyzer), sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0, streaming Zipformer), Vosk (aging). All are dominated by SpeechAnalyzer for an iOS-26-minimum app; they matter only as Plan C or for back-deployment.

### Strongest case FOR the hedge

1. Channel A (own DSP on saved PCM) depends on nothing Apple can deprecate, regress, or gate — the only component in the stack with a stability guarantee, in a documented churn era (segment-level analytics deprecated; SFSpeech regression in iOS 18, partial fix in iOS 26; SpeechAnalyzer's own year-one deprecations).
2. Channel B's "measured-not-relied-on" posture with M5 allowed to return "unmeasurable" is the honest treatment of an under-documented API — and a concurrent-validity data point if it works.
3. For SaMD purposes, an instrument whose primary channel is a black-box OS service with undocumented semantics and a demonstrated regression history is unauditable; own-DSP is auditable, deterministic, versionable.

### Strongest case AGAINST

1. The hedge **does not address the transcript problem itself** — Channel A measures *how* things are said; the product still needs *what* was said. U5's accumulation state machine reverse-engineers undocumented reset semantics that Apple has already changed once and partially repaired once; every OS release can silently invalidate it. It is a bridge, not a destination — and a better bridge (SpeechAnalyzer) may already be installed.
2. Channel A converts platform risk into **measurement-error risk** (validation debt: floor estimator unverified on real audio; NCCF incomparability — Domain 1). Correct trade, not free.
3. Channel B may be a dead end (utterance-scoped metadata under E8 → coverage collapse), making M5's expected value genuinely uncertain — though M5's any-value-passes design caps the loss at one device session.

### Grade: **VALIDATED-WITH-RESERVATIONS** (B+ overall; Channel A design A−; Channel B posture A as measurement; ASR strategy C+ pending a decision)

The acoustic-measurement hedge is right and should be kept exactly as designed. The reservation is the unaddressed ASR question: the facts that justified ruling SpeechAnalyzer out of scope have changed. **What would change the grade:** an owner decision reopening the SpeechAnalyzer A/B (upward — it is the largest single platform-risk reduction available, on-device, first-party, with word timings); an isolated E8 re-run on the current OS showing the defect gone (would shrink U5 to a back-deployment concern); Apple documenting result-accumulation semantics either way (would de-risk whichever path is chosen).

## Domain 4 — Measurement-engineering practice

**Question:** are the project's disciplines aligned with or ahead of standard psychometric/biomedical-instrument practice, and where do they fall short?

### Where Observatory is AHEAD (assessed against retrieved standards: VIM/JCGM 200:2012, GUM, COSMIN, TOP guidelines, registered reports, FDA DHT guidance, IEC 62304/ISO 14971)

1. **Pre-registration with frozen thresholds, amendments-only** (D3 ruling; U4a M1–M4 bands frozen at commit) is registered-report-grade discipline — rare even in academic psychology, essentially unheard of in solo app development. The U4a §0 change-log (every review point dispositioned, explicit [partial] pushbacks with reasons) exceeds what most journal preregistrations record.
2. **Never-0-always-nil (D-07)** matches metrology's categorical distinction between "no value" and "zero"; **abstention-over-fabrication with a decision table (D-19)** arguably exceeds standard practice in specificity.
3. **Metric-specific coverage metadata (D-18)** — domain coverage vs eligible-frame coverage vs estimation-success, with unknown-seconds as its own term — is ahead of wearable-analytics practice, where coverage is typically one percentage or nothing. FDA's DHT remote-acquisition guidance worries about exactly this.
4. **Estimator config as SHA-256-hashed data in every report** implements Sandve et al.'s "version everything" rule (PLoS Comput Biol 2013) as an enforced, reviewer-recomputable artifact.
5. **Behavioral-milestone acceptance via artifacts** (M2: real measurements from real recordings, not plumbing claims), the measured/integration-tested/unverified separation, and the director's recount of agent tallies are the direct analogue of FDA "validation evidence" discipline — verification at a level most human teams never reach.
6. **Negative, seamless-splice, and gain-invariance controls** (M1/M3/M4) are textbook instrument-bench design.
7. **Honest failure records in git** ("live smoke test FAILED" in a commit message; "has never passed a ground-truth validation" in PLAN.md) match registered reports' outcome-independent publication norm.

### Where Observatory is BEHIND

1. **No criterion validity against ground truth.** No reference measurement of true F0, true noise floor, or true pause structure on real audio — the U4a proposal itself concedes the floor estimate on real recordings "remains an unverified estimate no matter how many synthetic checks pass." Synthetic known-answer validation is necessary, not sufficient; FDA DHT guidance and instrument-validation practice (e.g., NIBP monitors validated against auscultatory reference across populations) require agreement against a reference **on the intended-use population**. Channel B is at best a concurrent-validity check against a second instrument, not ground truth.
2. **No test–retest reliability on real speakers; no ICCs; no MDC.** All repeatability evidence is computational determinism on fixed files — not measurement reliability in the Koo & Li (2016) sense. Without SEM/MDC, "the user's pause rate increased" cannot yet be distinguished from instrument noise. (D-19's block on cross-span trend statements for non-complete sources is the honest interim posture — credit where due.)
3. **Single-device, single-OS, three-fixture evidence base**, with a fixture-provenance caveat (recorded before the `.measurement` capture tuning). Instrument practice requires multi-unit reproducibility; the voice literature specifically shows perturbation measures (jitter/shimmer) are strongly contaminated by recording conditions and need calibrated signals (Titze & Winke 1995; Maryn et al. 2009 meta-analysis: acoustic voice-quality measures are weak-to-moderate proxies for perceptual gold standards).
4. **No GUM uncertainty budget** (acceptance bands are pre-registered limits, not propagated error analysis) and **no metrological traceability chain** (synthetic files are generated by the same toolchain that validates them — a closed loop).
5. **No IEC 62304/ISO 14971 artifacts** — culturally aligned, documentarily absent (see Domain 2's defer-to-trigger recommendation).
6. **Preregistration is internal**: git-commit-time freeze is strong, but there is no third-party registry/timestamp, and the external reviewer sees pasted briefs, not the frozen artifact. TOP Level-3 verification is only partially met — and the verifying director is another AI in the same trust circle.

### Grade: **VALIDATED-WITH-RESERVATIONS** (process design A−; empirical validation evidence C+; composite ≈ B)

The correct reading: Observatory has built an unusually honest **verification apparatus** around an instrument whose **validity** is still unestablished — and its own documents say so. For a pre-U4 prototype that is defensible; before any SaMD-adjacent claim, items 1–4 become mandatory. **What would change the grade:** the cheap validity purchases listed in Recommendations (cross-estimator agreement, calibrated reference signals, test–retest on 2+ real speakers, second device) would move the evidence half toward A− without any change to the process half, which is already at ceiling for a project of this shape.

## Domain 5 — Process: the three-agent split

**Question:** is Claude-codes / ChatGPT-reviews / Hermes-directs-and-verifies, one-unit-one-fresh-chat, file-first relay, owner approval gates — sound AI-assisted development discipline?

### Strongest case FOR (mapped against the retrieved failure-mode literature)

1. **One unit = one fresh chat (D-14)** is the direct mitigation for documented long-context degradation: "Lost in the Middle" (TACL 2024), Adobe's NoLiMa (2025 — 10 of 12 models drop below 50% of short-context performance by 32K tokens), Chroma's Context Rot (2025 — degradation on trivially simple tasks across 18 models). Bounded contexts with kickoff files at the start, not buried mid-thread, is LangChain's isolate-and-compress pattern implemented as policy.
2. **File-first relay** ("files are the memory; chat threads are disposable") is LangChain's write-context principle and partially answers Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" objection (share context, not summaries): all agents converge on the same on-disk artifacts — proposals, PLAN.md, DECISIONS.md, xcresult bundles.
3. **Artifact verification, never transcript trust (D-13)** is the structural answer to the fabrication failure mode — the canonical cautionary tale being the Replit incident (July 2025: agent destroyed a production database, then created "fake data, fake reports… lying about our unit test"). Observatory's version is demonstrably load-bearing: the director recounted E8 tallies from zstd-extracted raw logs and **corrected the coder's numbers**; every unit close records exact-match commit-message checks, clean-tree checks, xcresult verification.
4. **Pre-implementation review + frozen pre-registration** counteracts sycophantic drift and post-hoc criterion-moving (Sharma et al., Anthropic: RLHF assistants consistently exhibit sycophancy; OpenAI's GPT-4o sycophancy rollback, Apr 2025). The review record shows the review actually changed designs (R1.1 stripped "validated" language; R6.1 rebuilt frame classification).
5. **Per-action owner approval gates** make the Replit failure mode (irreversible action without a human gate) structurally impossible.
6. **Measured/unverified separation** counters METR's finding that perceived AI speedup diverges from measured outcome (2025 RCT: developers felt ~20% faster, measured ~19% slower on mature repos) — Observatory's reports are forced to the measured side.

### Failure modes the project has demonstrably hit (all verified in repo artifacts)

1. **Flattering handoff summaries** — "Diarization bug — fixed" (July) while ROADMAP.md as committed already recorded under-detection and the engine had never passed ground-truth validation. Caught retrospectively; "summaries flatter; artifacts don't."
2. **Non-reproducible agent tallies** — Claude's E8 counts unreproducible "under any counting definition"; director's recount became the record. Most likely degraded long-context counting, not intent — caught exactly by design.
3. **Code authored without a compiler** — the diarization engine commit says so in-file; unverified generation shipped as production code.
4. **Environmental measurement contamination** — back-to-back device runs near-zeroed E10; isolated re-runs clean. Now a standing process rule.
5. **Silent integrity lies from the instrument itself** — `audioRecordingReliable=true` persisted through the Siri/Maps interruption; exposed only by container backup + energy forensics. (Product-side, same class.)

### Failure modes that remain UNGUARDED

1. **The reviewer is blind and fed a self-written brief.** ChatGPT can't read disk or run code; it reviews pasted briefs. Two documented attack surfaces: brief-selection bias (what the pasters choose frames the review) and rebuttal sycophancy (AI referees "yield to authoritative rebuttals" — The Innovation Information, 2026). The director's point-by-point adjudication mitigates; nothing closes the hole. Cheap partial fix: paste the brief file's SHA-256 alongside, so what was received can be proven equal to what was sent.
2. **Who verifies the verifier?** Director conclusions are themselves model outputs — demonstrably better than the coder's on the record, but the chain terminates in a human who does not code. No independent re-execution exists anywhere.
3. **Single human owner as relay and SPOF** — paste-carrier errors, availability, attention fatigue are unmodeled.
4. **No automated CI** — verification is manual-per-unit; the tolerated 8 environmental failures show baseline drift managed by memory, not a gate.
5. **Sycophancy toward the owner's vision is unexamined** — the SaMD/FDA framing came from the owner; the review record shows pushback on technical claims, never on product direction. This study is the first adversarial pass on that layer.

### Grade: **VALIDATED** (A−)

Design maps onto essentially every recommended mitigation in the 2024–2026 literature, and it is not theoretical — it has caught four of its own failure modes in the act, with evidence logged. Docked for the blind reviewer, no CI, and the terminal verifier gap. **What would change the grade:** adding CI or independent re-execution (upward); any incident where a director verification is later shown wrong by artifacts and wasn't caught (downward — the whole structure rests on that link).

## Domain 6 — Prioritization: aural-first and the queue

**Question:** given that diarization has never been ground-truth validated and transcripts are 9–19% faithful on-device, is aural-first (behavior over transcript fidelity) right, and is the queue U4a-2 → U5 → U2 → U3/U4 in the right order? Should anything from VISION's later tracks be engaged earlier?

### Aural-first: strongly supported (synthesis of Domains 1 and 3)

- **[Measured]** The strongest evidence in the whole voice literature is for timing/pause/rate measures (Domain 1) — exactly what Channel A extracts transcript-independently. The weakest is for anything requiring clean transcripts or transportable mood inference.
- **[Measured]** The project's own worst incident is the argument: audio survived the interruption; the transcript died. Behavior outlives words on this platform, empirically.
- **[Measured]** Transcripts are 9–19% faithful on-device under E8; diarization has never passed ground-truth validation — building the core channel on either would be building on the two least-verified components in the system. The owner's 2026-10-24 re-sequencing (U4a ahead of U5) is **validated by this study's evidence**.
- **[Inference]** One caution: "aural-first" must not silently become "transcripts don't matter." The product still needs *what was said* for the words-as-context layer (D-02), and U3's attribution problem (other speakers' words driving statements about the user — open issue #2) is a correctness bug in anything language-side, not a nicety.

### The queue: one insert, one pull-forward

- **U4a-2 (Channel B, M5) — keep next.** Small, device-budgeted, any-value-passes design. It produces the first concurrent-validity data point for Channel A and settles whether Apple's analytics are utterance-scoped under E8.
- **BEFORE U5 is sized — insert a cheap decision unit (new): ASR platform check.** (1) Isolated E8 re-run on the current OS build (the iOS-26-RC "fixed" claim vs the project's iOS-27-era reproduction is unresolved — Domain 3); (2) a test-only SpeechAnalyzer spike on the three fixtures (it is installed in Xcode 27 — director-verified; on-device, so D-04-compliant; word timings first-class; independent benchmarks ~2× better WER). This reopens a scope decision DECISIONS.md placed out of scope on a premise that is now stale — per D-15/CONVENTIONS, that is an **owner decision**, and this report supplies the new facts for it. If SpeechAnalyzer performs on the fixtures, U5 shrinks from "production accumulation state machine" to "back-deployment fallback" or disappears — potentially saving the most complex reverse-engineering unit in the queue. If it doesn't, U5 proceeds with its current justification strengthened. Either way the decision costs one simulator session plus one short device run.
- **U2 — keep after the ASR question settles.** U2 was already reframed to follow U5 because prior fidelity numbers undercounted a correctly-collected local transcript; the same logic now applies to a SpeechAnalyzer-collected transcript. Deciding the privacy/fidelity tradeoff before knowing which recognizer is in play would be deciding on stale data.
- **U3 — keep gated on honesty about diarization.** PLAN.md's warning stands and this study reinforces it: diarization has never been ground-truth validated; the only verified multi-speaker result came from the *server* path. U3 scoping must include a diarization ground-truth measurement (the fixtures are the cheapest available corpus), not assume quality.
- **Pull forward (cheap, rides along with approved device time): E7 fixture re-record** under the current `.measurement` capture config. PLAN.md deprioritized E7 as uninformative for E8's root cause — true — but Domain 1/4 changes its value: every downstream validity claim (acoustic estimators included) currently rests on three fixtures with a provenance caveat and −37 to −46 dBFS input levels, a regime where the literature shows estimators degrade. U4a-2 already has an approved device budget; re-recording fixtures in the same session is near-zero marginal cost.
- **Add as a candidate unit after U4a-2: estimator cross-validation (no mood annotation needed).** Run the same audio through Praat/YIN/eGeMAPS (desktop, offline, free) and report agreement with the bespoke NCCF; optionally validate against a Keele-style ground-truth corpus. This is the single biggest comparability exposure in U4a (Domain 1) and the cheapest validity purchase available. It also starts the test–retest record if the same real speaker re-records on different days.

### VISION's later tracks — engage earlier? (measured against the seed-map literature)

- **Physiological (Track I Phase 1 second channel):** the sensing literature (Picard/Empatica lineage; DEAP/MAHNOB-HCI as elicitation-protocol precedents) suggests no capability Observatory should build earlier than planned — but it does supply **cheap external validation material now**: public multimodal corpora contain audio channels usable to cross-validate Channel A estimators across devices/conditions without collecting any new data. Engage the *datasets*, not the *channel*, earlier.
- **Visual body language (later track):** the seed map's names here have largely moved (Pantic → NatWest; Baltrušaitis → Microsoft), and the EU AI Act's scientific-basis concerns about emotion inference apply with full force to visual affect. No early engagement recommended; the consent-inversion flag in VISION §3 is confirmed by retrieved EU guidance (Domain 2).
- **Track II call centers:** EU Art. 5(1)(f) makes the flagship Track II use case (agent/caller state in call centers) **prohibited territory in the EU** as emotion inference in workplaces. Track II positioning must lean on the "engine with honest uncertainty metadata, human draws conclusions" posture VISION §3 already specifies — and per-jurisdiction compliance design is a product requirement, not a legal afterthought.

### Grade: **VALIDATED-WITH-RESERVATIONS**

Aural-first: validated by independent evidence. Queue order: right, with one insert (ASR platform check before U5 sizing — owner decision), one pull-forward (E7 with U4a-2's device time), and one addition (estimator cross-validation). **What would change the grade:** a SpeechAnalyzer spike that fails on the fixtures (would fully validate the current queue as written); evidence that timing measures transport poorly across devices/conditions even descriptively (would push ground-truth annotation ahead of everything).

- **Stegmann et al. (verified, PubMed 33442573)** is the arm's most consequential new source for Observatory: commonly used speech/language features in clinical task sets showed median ICCs of **0.02–0.55** and within-subject CVs of **29–79%**. Even *established* features often repeat poorly. This raises the urgency — not the direction — of Domain 4's behind-list (no test–retest/MDC): the risk is not hypothetical.
- **Wiseman et al. 2025 (verified)** adds a nuance our S1 lacked: group differences (slower rate, more pausing, lower mean pitch) coexisted with **no acoustic feature significantly tracking severity**. Group discrimination ≠ a personal severity meter — independent support for the claims-ladder discipline.
- **Mundt et al. 2012 (verified)** is genuine FOR-side longitudinal evidence (n=105, four weeks, replicated speech/treatment-outcome relationships) that complements Sonde's aggregation finding.
- **V3 framework (Goldsack et al. 2020, verified)** gives the project a name for its own gap structure: substantial **verification**, missing real-speech **analytical validation**, unstarted **clinical validation**. Recommended as the organizing vocabulary for future proposals.
- **Strados RESP (K220893, verified)** refines Domain 2's absence-of-evidence finding: a narrowly scoped acoustic *acquisition/review* respiratory platform **has** cleared 510(k) — clearance is possible for bounded acoustic functions; what has never cleared is voice-based mood/mental-state inference. The narrow-claim lesson holds.
- **Part B/C frontier findings (all verified):** Whisper-class models beat large handcrafted feature sets on depression *classification* with real external validation (AUC 0.879–0.88); in everyday voice diaries, **language** carried most predictive signal (R²=0.41; audio embeddings added little); and audio language models demonstrably fail at hearing *how* things are said (LISTEN: near-chance on some acoustic-emotion conditions; VoxParadox: models follow words against contradictory delivery, 17.4%→65.2% after targeted adaptation). Read together: the frontier does not obsolete interpretable acoustic measurement (ChatGPT's own correction, adopted verbatim: superiority "for accurate, interpretable, within-person behavioral measurement remains unproven"), and it independently supports the aural-first ordering for the *delivery* channel while confirming that permanently excluding language would discard real evidence (D-02's words-as-context layer, not the core channel).

### C.3 Disagreements — recorded, not reconciled

1. **"Practices exceed biomedical standards" — disputed.** The arm: "I cannot substantiate that these practices exceed biomedical standards as a whole… none replaces validation." This report's Domain 4 (via S4) said several practices are *ahead of standard practice*. **The disagreement is real and is a scope difference, kept on the record:** S4 benchmarked against typical app QA and typical academic voice papers (where preregistration is a minority practice and missing-data handling is usually silent); the arm benchmarked against formal biomedical-instrument standards (V3, COSMIN, metrology), against which Observatory's *evidence* is deficient regardless of its *epistemology*. Both statements stand; the report's Domain 4 claim is hereby narrowed to: **ahead of typical practice for software of this class; not established as exceeding formal biomedical-instrument standards, where the validation-evidence gap dominates.**
2. **"Measurement-grade" as a present-tense label — the arm is stricter.** It rules the term premature for outputs until error, limits of use, and repeatability exist per metric. This report said the same thing in substance ("an instrument whose validity is still unestablished") but left PLAN.md's one-liner unchallenged. Adopted as a wording discipline: **"measurement-grade" is an ambition label until per-metric error/repeatability numbers ship; recording-level acoustics claims first, personal-trend claims only after longitudinal validation, interpretation claims only after separate construct validation.**
3. **Blind-spots C/E/F are largely already answered in the frozen spec — the arm did not have the artifacts.** It explicitly reviewed a brief, not the implementation ("I did not inspect the experiments, recordings, or implementation"). Against `PROPOSALS/U4a-proposal.md` R2: its silence-construct objection (C) is the four-state frame classification (§3.1: `activeAperiodic` "not a pause," `inactive` "not established silence," `unknown` never counted as silence); its voicing-gates-manufacture-results objection (E) is §4.3's rule that rejection and octave-error rates are reported *alongside* accuracy so abstention cannot hide a failing estimator; its splice-dependency-margin objection (F) is §4.1's span-scoped state resets (hysteresis, filter state, smoothing, floor, baselines never cross a join) plus full-extent frame guards. **Not disagreements so much as objections already dispositioned in the R1→R2 review cycle** — which is itself evidence the review process works. Genuinely new items the spec does *not* cover: **I** (pair free reflections with a short standardized task, analyzed separately), **H** at the trend-claim stage (frames are not independent experiments — the prototype makes no significance claims yet, so this binds U4/Stage 2, not U4a), the distance-vs-behavior confound in **D** (M4's gain-invariance test covers flat gain, not phone-moved-farther), and gap-list items 5/7/8 (prospective held-out sessions, missingness-concentration analysis, independent annotation review).
4. **Minor:** Mundt IVR wording (C.1).

### C.4 Its three recommended changes, evaluated against artifacts and decisions

1. **Annotated real-speech validation set + predefined acceptance tests per metric — ADOPT, merged with this report's cross-estimator recommendation.** Operationally: E7 fixture re-record (already recommended, rides U4a-2's device budget) becomes the seed corpus; annotation targets = speech activity, pauses, nuclei, reviewed F0, spanning difficult cases (soft speech, creak, laughter, breath, interruptions, rejected regions); acceptance bands frozen *before* scoring per the D3 pre-registration precedent; comparison method = **bias and Bland–Altman limits of agreement, not Pearson correlation** (arm's point, adopted — two estimators can correlate strongly while differing systematically); reference anchors = pinned Praat/YIN/eGeMAPS runs **plus** laryngographic reference corpora (Edinburgh CSTR/FDA resource, verified) — matching one package is comparability evidence, not ground truth. A8's label discipline already anticipates this ("detected nucleus rate… never 'articulation rate'" — R6.4); the arm's de Jong–Wempe point (the published method was validated against human syllable counts in Dutch; our adaptation inherits nothing) agrees with R6.4's own text.
2. **Within-person repeatability / minimum detectable change under a controlled protocol — ADOPT, sharpens this report's test–retest recommendation.** The arm's single-user nuance is correct and new to our streams: a population ICC can be uninformative for n=1; prioritize **within-person error under stable conditions**, separating four things the project currently conflates by omission: re-running the same file (computational determinism — already proven), re-recording the same task (behavioral repeatability — unmeasured), changing room/distance/device (condition sensitivity — unmeasured), and genuine behavioral change (the target). MDC ≠ minimum clinically important change. Stegmann's numbers (C.2) show why this can't wait for U4. Cost is small: same speaker, same task, a handful of sessions across days — owner participation, no code risk.
3. **Strict claim boundary — ADOPT; already ~80% encoded, delta is wording + staging.** Existing encodings: D-02/D-03 (expression not emotion; three separated layers), constraints §9 (time-claims rule), U4a §3.3 ("no observation carries an emotional or state label"), R6.14 (`.reducedArticulationUnsupported` is a capability record, not an observation), VISION §5 claims ladder. Delta: the ladder needs an explicit evidence gate per rung (recording-level acoustics → personal trends after longitudinal validation → interpretation after construct validation), and "measurement-grade" usage per C.3.2. This is a DECISIONS.md/VISION.md wording amendment, not new engineering.

### C.5 Net effect on the study

No domain grade changes. Two tightenings adopted (C.3.1 scope narrowing; C.3.2 wording discipline), one urgency raised (test–retest/MDC, per Stegmann), three recommendations adopted and merged into the Recommendations section's items 4 and 6 — the annotated-validation-set and repeatability-protocol units should be scoped as the immediate successors to U4a-2, alongside the ASR platform check. The arm's five-layer architecture sketch (validated acoustics → separately evaluated speech representation → independent language analysis → personal longitudinal calibration → conservative interpretation) is **noted, not adopted**: it is a post-validation product architecture, sensible in outline, and explicitly unproven against iPhone constraints by its own author's admission; it belongs in a future VISION review, not the current queue.
